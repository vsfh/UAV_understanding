\begin{abstract}
Low-altitude UAV inspection requires recognizing operational events rather than merely naming visible objects: garbage may be floating or waterside, materials may be useful stock or construction waste, and smoke may indicate emission or fire. These decisions require scene context and small local evidence. We introduce \dataset, a development snapshot toward a real-world benchmark with a compositional event ontology and two linked tasks: context--crop event recognition and context-level multi-evidence set prediction. A content audit reconciles 9,093 pair annotations across 45 present concepts into 7,279 exact-hash-unique wide contexts, 9,056 unique crops, and 9,082 unique context--crop pairs. Crucially, 1,389 contexts link multiple evidence crops and 269 contexts carry multiple event labels; we preserve this structure as a \samplegraph rather than misreporting every pair as an independent frame. We recover 1,549 unique class-level business record IDs, but zero can yet be deterministically joined to a local image. A normalized source-record/media/context gate therefore blocks the official split and acquisition-level claims instead of laundering filename dates into flights or sites. The benchmark separates a support-gated core from long-tail, open-vocabulary, and hard-negative tracks, with independent human-review ledgers and verified acquisition provenance as release gates. We further propose \methodfull (\method), a parameter-efficient VLM baseline with graph-neighbor rejection and a permutation-invariant set extension. Its protocol tests whether joint predictions truly require complementary views through single-view insufficiency, assigned-evidence deletion, prompt paraphrases, and crop-budget diagnostics. This draft reports verified audit facts and a frozen evaluation design; model results remain \tbd until the fail-closed gate is ready and three-seed runs are complete.
\end{abstract}
